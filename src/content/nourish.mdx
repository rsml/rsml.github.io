## My GLP-1 app

After following Nourish for years, I just applied for your <a href="https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/usenourish/jobs/5156632008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Staff Software Engineer role</a>. As CTO and co-founder, I spent two years building Vivy ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6736508784)), a GLP-1 companion app with an AI coach woven into nearly every feature. We grew to 10,000 paid users, a 4.8 star rating, and 77% month-1 retention.

## What went wrong

Vivy had product market fit, measured by that month-1 retention. But we never found distribution market fit. We couldn't get our CAC low enough, didn't build enough organic traffic, and ultimately did not have enough resources to build a B2B motion.

## What worked

People came back for a few reasons. They wanted to hit their daily goals and close their rings. They wanted their questions answered, by the coach and by our private peer community. They also wanted to share their wins and connect with others on the same journey, because a lot of people on GLP-1s feel isolated or stigmatized.

Our community was one of the most loved parts of the app. It added accountability, peer support, and a variable reward, since something new could always be waiting. It also gave us a reason to send one good notification a day, namely the top thread of the day. I built smart widgets people could share on their profile and in community posts, like a photo gallery, goal progress, and water intake.

## What Nourish has that Vivy didn't

Distribution is the one thing I never cracked at Vivy, and it's your deepest moat. You have the largest dietitian network in the country, it's covered by insurance, and health systems refer patients straight into it. At Vivy, we were purely B2C. Due in large part to the special restrictions on ads in the health category on social media, our CAC was simply too high.

## What I'd bring

I'm a very hardworking developer who's constantly working on improving my skills and cares about lifting up people around me. In this time of rapid change in how software is built, I've been spending a lot of time learning how to move fast in a HIPAA compliant codebase through the use of a carefully built AI harness.

I'm an unusual developer in that I also have a lot of experience approaching problems from the lens of a product designer, a UX designer, a CTO, and an entrepreneur who needs to ultimately solve the business's needs.

Furthermore, I spent two years building for people on GLP-1s and am deeply familiar with the needs of this population. I've also done MNT myself as a patient, and am familiar with the struggles of navigating the healthcare industry.

## My next move

My mission has always been to help people through software. I've decided that for the rest of my career I want to forego entrepreneurship, where I have had to split my time a hundred ways, and instead work inside existing companies where I can focus more narrowly on crafting great software that can positively impact millions of people. That is why I'd love to be a part of Nourish's mission to help everyone live a healthier, longer, and happier life.

If any of this resonates, I'd love to meet with you: [https://cal.com/rossnmiller/meeting](https://cal.com/rossnmiller/meeting).
